# Seer Agent v2: causal replay core (Slice 1)

`seer_agent.v2` is the first slice of the v2 architecture: one owner, bounded inputs, explicit coverage and a reducer that never reads the future. It sits next to the 0.2 runtime and does not change it. Slice 2 adds the scheduler and the Intention, Question and Episode records; Slice 3 connects a real worker.

## Modules

| Module | Holds | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `clock.py` | `CausalClock`, `CausalViolation` | The replay cutoff in milliseconds. Every reader calls `check(available_at_ms)`; a read past the cutoff raises `CausalViolation`, an `AssertionError` |
| `records.py` | `Frame`, `Watch`, `InferenceRequest`, `InferenceResult`, `Trace`, `RESULT_SCHEMA`, `PRIORITIES` | Values only. A frame carries `captured_ms` and `published_ms`; a watch fixes predicate, candidate set (always including `insufficient_visibility`), threshold, margin and freshness |
| `source.py` | `BoundedSource`, `EvidenceLost` | Ring buffer for one source session. Duplicates are dropped, frames are listed in capture order after their publish time until the core acknowledges them, unpinned frames leave after `retention_ms` or when the ring is full; a ring full of pinned frames drops the new frame and says so |
| `worker.py` | `Worker`, `FakeWorker` | Workers return data and latency. The fake worker follows a script keyed by the requested interval and counts its calls |
| `store.py` | `ReplayStore` | SQLite tables for owners, watches per revision, frames, coverage, evidence, manifests, jobs, attempts, situations, alternatives, observations, estimates, checks, questions, traces and the search index. Guarded readers: `frames`, `evidence`, `result`, `coverage`, `search`; read models filter by committed time; every transaction is fenced on the committed cutoff and, once a core owns the store, on the latest owner epoch (a bare `ReplayStore` is fenced on the cutoff only) |
| `core.py` | `ReplayCore` | The only writer: intake, requests, job runs, recovery and the six-step reducer |

## Causal contract

Time is integer milliseconds on the replay timeline. The harness advances `CausalClock`; the core never does.

- A frame observes the instant `captured_ms` and stands for the sampling period that ends there, so its coverage is `[captured_ms - period_ms, captured_ms]`. `published_ms >= captured_ms` is required, which is the research protocol's `end_s <= available_at_s`. `BoundedSource.readable` lists a frame only once `published_ms <= cutoff`, and `ReplayCore.ingest` refuses a frame published after the cutoff.
- The store persists the last committed cutoff (`meta.cutoff_ms`, updated on every commit). Opening it with a clock behind that value raises `CausalViolation`, so a successor cannot read commits from its own future; the read models also filter by committed time. Every transaction re-checks the cutoff after taking the write lock, so a stale owner with an older clock cannot commit either.
- Evidence handles are readable from `available_from_ms` (publish) to `available_until_ms` (publish plus retention) unless a job pinned them. Pinned bytes are capped by `pin_budget_bytes`. A request over the cap is refused with `pin_budget_full` and an open question `evidence_lost_before_inspection`; frames that expired before a look was requested are recorded the same way and the look proceeds on what remains. Ring pins are applied after the request committed, and `attach` makes the ring's pins match the store.
- A worker result becomes readable at `started_ms + latency_ms`. `reduce_pending` ignores results whose availability time is after the cutoff and `ReplayStore.result` raises before then.
- The search index is appended only by the reducer (step 3), and `search(until_ms)` refuses to look past the cutoff. Nothing precomputes the future.
- Coverage levels are `captured`, `signal` and `inspected`; `ReplayStore.coverage` returns explicit `unknown` segments for everything else. An unobserved interval never becomes an estimate, and a look never covers a gap between its frames: `inspected` is written per contiguous span of the frames actually read. Frame spans that touch or are apart by at most half a period (capture jitter) form one span; a larger gap stays unknown. `ReplayCore.coverage` reads coverage with the same tolerance, which bridges `signal` and `inspected` rows as well as capture spans; `ReplayStore.coverage` is exact.

## Owners, watches and jobs

Each `ReplayCore` takes an `owner_epoch` higher than every epoch the store has seen (the default is the next one); reusing or lowering it is refused, and so is a second core on the same `ReplayStore` object (`store_already_owned`): a successor opens its own store on the file. From then on every commit through that store is fenced: once a successor has taken a higher epoch, the superseded owner's next transaction fails with `owner_superseded` and nothing it still had in flight is written. Results committed under earlier epochs of the same store were therefore committed while that owner was current; they are the store's own prior work and are reduced normally. A result under an epoch the store never had is audited only (`audited:foreign_owner_epoch`), with no observation, estimate or index entry. Reducing an attempt that is already reduced is a no-op, never a rewrite.

Every watch revision's body is kept. A job records the revision it was requested under and is reduced with that revision's threshold and candidate set; if a newer revision exists by then, the result is filed as `history:stale_watch_revision` with its own decision rather than being re-scored or rejected.

`request(watch_id, from_ms, to_ms, deadline_ms=..., kind=..., priority=...)` pins the frames captured in the interval and queues one logical job. If the kept frames do not form one contiguous span (allowing capture jitter of at most half a period) the request is refused with `not_contiguous` and the segments, and each gap inside the request is recorded as a `not_observed` question. The logical key is a digest of watch, revision, kind and handles: a request is `known` while a job with that key is pending, running, stored or reduced with a valid result; a closed job (expired, failed, rejected) does not block the interval and a new job supersedes it.

`run_pending` runs one job at a time in fixed priority order (reserved coverage first, housekeeping last, earliest deadline within a class), records the worker's declared model id on the attempt at dispatch, stores the raw result and does not reduce it. A worker exception or an owner restart gives the job one more local attempt within its deadline (`MAX_ATTEMPTS = 2`). Output that cannot be stored (a NaN score, non-JSON values), output that is not a JSON object, and schema errors are results, not retry reasons; the first two are rejected when the result arrives and never reach the reducer. A partial unique index keeps one valid result per logical job. A job whose deadline passed before it started is expired with a `missed_deadline` question; a result that finishes after the deadline is reduced as history with the same question.

## The reducer

`reduce_pending` reduces each stored and available result in one transaction with six named steps, `_step1_validate` to `_step6_open_questions` in `core.py`:

1. Validate job identity, provenance (the result names the model that was dispatched), availability cutoff, quality (JSON schema, the requesting revision's candidate set, contiguous frames), one valid result per job, owner epoch and watch revision. Hard failures end as `rejected:<code>` or `audited:<code>`; a stale watch revision or a missed deadline continues as history.
2. Attach the result to the inspected frames' own capture spans and write `inspected` coverage per span. The time inference finished is never used for this.
3. Record the observation as support for the picked candidate and contradiction for the others. A second result over the same frame set is a repeat, not a new observation.
4. First demote a current estimate that has fallen out of `freshness_ms` (reason `expired`, `demoted_ms` set), whatever the new result says, including repeats and undecided looks. Then update the current estimate only when the result is applicable, not older than the current estimate's interval and within `freshness_ms`; otherwise store it as history with the reason (`stale_watch_revision`, `missed_deadline`, `older_than_current`, `stale`). A superseded estimate also records `demoted_ms`.
5. Keep every alternative of the latest candidate set and record why the situation revision changed.
6. For a result that became current: open a `recheck_next_frame` check when the top two scores are within `margin` and a `prior_free_verify` check when the hypothesis changed. For an undecided result (unknown or insufficient visibility): open a `not_decidable` question. History placements change no decision and open no check.

Every stored result ends in one `Trace`: source, session, sequence range, job and attempt, mission version, watch revision, context version (the committed snapshot the job was requested against), owner epoch of the request, worker model id, queue and inference time, evidence manifest reference and outcome. Expired and failed jobs get a trace with their last attempt number (0 if none) and an open question.

## Replay schedule

The reducer stamps `committed_ms`, `observed_ms` and `reduced_ms` with the cutoff of the tick that reduced the result, and judges freshness and expiry against that cutoff. Replay outcomes therefore depend on the tick schedule: a result available at 13000 that is first reduced at a 45000 tick is stale. This is not a causality violation, but a harness that wants outcomes independent of its step size should tick at each result's availability time (`run_pending` returns it). Two reads are worth knowing: the constructor reads `MAX(available_ms)` of unreduced attempts, a future time value, only as the worker's busy marker, never as evidence; and `_reduce` checks the result's availability against the cutoff before loading the raw result, which `reduce_pending` already filters on.

```sh
python -m venv .venv
.venv/bin/python -m pip install -e . pytest      # Windows: .venv\Scripts\python
PYTHONUTF8=1 .venv/bin/python -m pytest -q tests/v2
```

`tests/v2/test_v2_scenarios.py` holds the four Slice 1 scenarios (duplicate and out-of-order input, outage, restart before reduction, backlog) and the restart retry rule. `tests/v2/test_v2_contract.py` covers reads past the cutoff, the pin budget, retention loss, stale watch revisions, rejected results, questions and checks, and the bounded source. `tests/v2/test_v2_review.py` holds one regression per review finding: a look straddling an outage, re-requesting an expired job, a restart with a raised epoch, an added candidate in a newer revision, a successor with an earlier clock, freshness expiry, unserializable and non-object results, results finishing after the deadline, undeclared model ids, ring pin lifecycle, a superseded owner on a shared database, capture jitter and a rolled-back release. Fixtures are synthetic: one camera, one session, frames with a size and a label instead of pixels.

## Known limits of this slice

- A current estimate that falls out of its freshness window is demoted at the next reduction for that situation (any result, including repeats and undecided looks), not by a timer; until then `current_estimate` still returns it.
- The ring deduplicates only against frames it still holds; the store deduplicates durably by (source, session, sequence), so a re-sent evicted frame is listed again and ingested as a duplicate.
- One session per source is assumed when a job records its sequence range.
- Intention, the operator Question record, Episode, the budget ledger and admission queue, routine hypotheses, operator answers, delivery and the real worker belong to Slices 2 and 3. The bounded source keeps frames in memory; the client ring buffer and the `media.*` calls replace it in Slice 5.
